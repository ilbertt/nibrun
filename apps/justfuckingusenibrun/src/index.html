Just fucking use nibrun

Congratulations, champ. You compiled a binary. One file. It has your HTTP server in it, your templates in it, your SQLite driver in it. It runs on a laptop with the wifi off. And then you looked at that one perfect file and thought: you know what this needs? A cluster.

So now there's a Dockerfile that installs a runtime your binary doesn't fucking need. There's a registry. There's a YAML file you copied from a blog post in 2021 and have never once understood. There's a load balancer, balancing load across the one instance you have. There's a managed Postgres with a connection pooler in front of it, for the four hundred rows your app will ever hold. Your app has eleven users, asshole. Six of them are you, logged in on different browsers.

Your app needs two things. You are buying nine.

Here is the entire list of what a compiled binary requires to be a real, working, on-the-internet application:

  1. Somewhere to run.
  2. Somewhere to put files.

That's it. That's the list. Everything else on your invoice is ceremony you performed to feel like a Serious Engineer. You didn't need object storage, you needed a directory. You didn't need a build pipeline, you have a binary — the build already fucking happened. You didn't need horizontal scaling, you needed to stop lying to yourself about the traffic.

So here's the whole product, you overthinking bastard

Drop the binary. Get a machine and a disk. Done. No, there is no step three. Stop looking for step three.

  • A microVM of its very own. A real Firecracker machine, 1 vCPU and 256 MiB, and nothing else lives in it. Not a container squatting next to forty strangers. Your app, alone, like it wanted.
  • A data/ directory that doesn't fucking vanish. 8 GiB. Write a SQLite file to it. Write uploads to it. Write a text file called notes.txt to it. It survives every redeploy, because it's a disk, and that is what disks have always done.
  • An HTTPS URL the moment it boots. https://your-app.nibrun.app. No certificate ritual. No DNS spelunking. No ingress controller with opinions.
  • Your shit back, whenever you want it. One command and you get a tarball: the exact binary you uploaded, the entire disk as it sits, and the env vars it ran with. There is no managed database to migrate off, because there was never a managed database.

The deploy, in full, in its entirety

curl -fsSL https://nibrun.com/install.sh | sh
nib login
nib run ./my-server

Read it again. That's the deploy. Not the quickstart, not the toy example, not the "simplified for illustration" version — that is the actual thing you type to put your actual app on the actual internet. Shipping a new version is the same command again.

Can't be bothered to open a terminal? Fine, princess. Drag the binary onto app.nibrun.com with your mouse, like an animal. It works exactly the same.

All your binary has to do in return: be Linux x86_64, and listen on the port we hand you in PORT, on 0.0.0.0. That's the whole contract. It fits in a sentence because it is one.

When not to use it, since apparently we're the only honest ones here

One app, one machine. That means no horizontal scaling and no blue/green — a deploy stops the old VM and starts the new one, so there's a few seconds where your eleven users see nothing. The disk is local and unreplicated, so the export is the backup, and you should actually run it.

If your app genuinely has to be several machines at once, then congratulations, you have outgrown this, and you should go back to your YAML with our blessing. That's not a missing feature we're shipping next quarter. That's the fucking design. Small apps don't need to scale. They need a machine and a disk.
